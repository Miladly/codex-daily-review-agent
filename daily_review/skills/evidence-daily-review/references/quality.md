# Review quality rubric

Check these dimensions, in order. These instructions guide semantic review; passing structural validation alone cannot prove semantic accuracy.

1. **Work history:** The reader can recover what was handled, what action was taken, what output or stage resulted, and where the artifact/evidence is. Records are the main body; thoughtful prose cannot compensate for missing actual work. Preserve later repairs, provisional approvals and partial deliveries.
2. **Fidelity:** Every factual clause follows from its references. Plans, smoke tests, command success and assistant claims are not promoted to verified delivery or scientific conclusions. Separate uncertain explanations from established causes.
3. **Coverage and selection:** Preserve important early/middle outcomes as well as the latest state. Summarize repeated retries compactly. Do not claim full coverage when input selection, source availability or prior context is limited.
4. **Project progress:** Explain what changed and what remains uncertain. Prefer concrete artifacts, decisions and validation boundaries over counts of messages, commands or file edits. Do not rank projects by event volume.
5. **Continuity:** Prior context retains its date and scope. Link a carryover to today's result only with evidence of the actual relationship; otherwise keep it pending verification. Do not turn stale context into today's achievement.
6. **Actionability:** Next steps are supported, limited to three at the daily layer and have observable acceptance criteria. Suggestions are labeled as proposals, with no invented owner, deadline or promise.
7. **Human agency:** Preserve the user's reflection area. Do not invent personal feelings, hours, intent or offline work. Do not execute or publish review suggestions.

If evidence is insufficient, omit the claim or say the point remains unverified using supporting refs. Concise uncertainty is better than polished speculation.
